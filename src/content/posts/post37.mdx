I spent five years trying to prove I wasn’t a ghost. I succeeded. But by the time the world finally saw me, I had forgotten how to see myself.

### **Five Years of Total Concentration**


In 2020, I was rejected. It was personal. It hurt. But it did something else, too. It opened my eyes. I realized I was just a random person in a crowded world. I was invisible. I decided, right then, that I would never be invisible again. I wanted to be undeniable.

![The Start](/images/posts/37a.jpg)
**I locked my door. I disappeared.**

For two years, I disappeared into making games. The work felt like a playground then: pure, an escape. I was not only making games; I was making a version of myself that did not need anyone else. I spent months adjusting how light moved across a scene. I obsessed over [models of the moon](https://youtu.be/RJW3zgzZl3w). I learned enough to feel like I could bring the moon into the palm of my hand. I got lost in the patterns behind the world I was trying to make.

In 2022, I joined college. My first year was not about the fests. It was not about making friends. It was about my own projects. I built a tool of my own that could read a small language I designed. It was fast. While others were learning the basic rules, I was deciding how the tool itself should understand them. I stopped being only a student. I became a [builder](https://github.com/SujalChoudhari).

![The Machine](/images/posts/37b.jpg)
**Then, the rhythm changed. The "Machine" took over.**

December 2023 was my first major win at a national competition. That was the spark. By February 2024, I was in a state of total concentration. I won a competition every single weekend. Four weeks. Four trophies. I was moving at a pace that left people breathless.

But in May 2024, the weight of that speed finally cracked the team.

We had to remove one person. Another left shortly after. It was messy. The worst part was that they took our work with them. They took our information and our hard work to start a rival team. Suddenly, we were not just competing against the world; we were competing against our own shadows. **Shivers.** It was a cold, heavy realization.

But work is only a snapshot. It is a ghost of where we used to be. We were the ones who could improve, while they were stuck with what we had already made. We evolved. They did not. We beat them anyway.

![Competition](/images/posts/37c.jpg)
[**The wins kept coming. The work got deeper.**](https://sujal.xyz)


I wrote books. I shared tools that other people could use. I won again at the national level. In the summer of 2025, I worked at a finance startup. I built systems that watched activity as it happened. They liked my work. They offered me a full-time job.

![Wins](/images/posts/37d.jpg)
**I said no.**

I was still chasing the peak. October 2025 was placement season. I interviewed with a major international bank. Then I waited. One month of silence passed. During that month, another company came to campus. They moved fast. They offered me a job. I took it. I was officially "placed."

Then the truth came out. The bank finally reached out; they wanted me. But the college administration stepped in. Because I was already placed at the other company, they told the bank no. They didn't ask me. They didn't care about what I wanted. They just closed the door on my behalf. Five years of work, and I was still just a checkbox on a spreadsheet.

By December 2025, I reached the peak. I became the lead for a project exploring new forms of computer intelligence at a top startup accelerator. I had made it. I was not a random person anymore.

![Placement](/images/posts/37e.jpg)
**But I don't recommend this.**


I burned five years of my youth to prove a point to a world that does not care about the fire. I lost the social skills that make life worth living. I stayed in my room until the fire to prove myself was the only thing keeping me warm. I won the trophies, but I lost the ability to just exist.

---

### **The New Rule**

2026 is about reclaiming my humanity.

![The Cost](/images/posts/37f.jpg)

I am done being a utility. For years, I was a problem-solving machine that did not need a social life. If you had a problem, you called me. If you had something difficult to finish, I was the one who finished it. That version of me is dead.

My new rule is simple: **be more human, and be more mean.**

Being "mean" is not about being a jerk. It is about boundaries. It is about saying "no" to people who only value me for my output. Free tools exist; my time is not.

I finally proved I am not a ghost. Now, I am going to find out if I am any good at being a person. The screen is off. The door is open. I am going to live.

See ya.
