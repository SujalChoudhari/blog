I keep saying I am writing a book, but that is not quite how it feels. I am building a world, and the book is the part of it I have chosen to show. *When the Gods Went Silent* follows a narrow route through Avantika, from Indrakatha to Tapovan, through the ash country and toward the old ashrams. But each place had to exist before the characters arrived. Avantika is an old empire shaped by trade and scholarship. Indrakatha once carried traces of that older culture. Under Agnimitra, it becomes an industrial city of iron, smoke, surveillance, and function. Even a carved stone at street level or a sealed house in a plague village can carry more history than the novel has room to explain.

Magic had to work the same way. The three Puranas are not just books of spells. They hold different kinds of physical knowledge: Shrijan, Dhriti, and Samhara. Creation is tied to structure. Preservation is tied to bodies, balance, and force. Destruction is tied to anatomy and the points where a thing fails. That is why Vaidehi's life in Tapovan matters before she understands any of this. Salt, water, terrain, breath, and weight are not decorative details in her past. They are the first language of the system she later enters.

The world also needed a history of power. Who was allowed to study the Puranas? Why did sacred knowledge remain inside a few institutions and families? What happens to ordinary people when prayers stop receiving an answer? The silence of the gods changes more than the supernatural parts of the story. It changes education, politics, religion, and the meaning of authority. A ruler can use that silence to present himself as the only person capable of restoring order. The novel touches these questions through cities, temples, and characters, but the worldbuilding has to hold the larger consequences.

A lot of the world never appears directly in the book. There are regions of Avantika that the characters never visit, histories that exist only to shape a place, and consequences that are felt without receiving a full explanation. I do not see that as wasted work. A world does not become believable because the author explains everything. It becomes believable when the visible details feel connected to something larger. I am not writing a book and filling in a blank page. I am building a world, then choosing one story that can live inside it.
